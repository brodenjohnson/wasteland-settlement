## The one-line version

You are the only person alive who can wake three thousand sleepers, and you chose to forget how.

## Tone

Quiet, dry, human. The world is dead but nobody out here is a chosen one. You are a person who made a hard, loving decision and then erased yourself so nobody could talk you out of it, including you. Humour comes from understatement. The horror is mostly missing information. Nobody explains anything in a speech.

## What the player knows after chapter one

- You woke in the valley with a note in your own handwriting. It says it worked, you remember nothing, and that was the plan.
- Ten other survivors wear numbered tags, 1 to 10. You hold tag 11. Each of them has a fragment and none of them has the whole thing.
- The dispatch slip says eleven went in and eleven came out, and that Unit 11 was to be collected last. A twelfth line on a manifest was crossed out in thick black ink.
- The pump ledger says the register has eleven, the keeper counted ten in the ruins, and one is missing. The keeper knows which.
- The tape at the Ash Tower says you asked to have this done to you. It says the coast is next, and to bring the seeds and the ten. Its last lines are "Do not trust the radio. That is us. That is the radio."
- The ledger also says: take the tag, do not take the stairs.

## What actually happened (spoilers)

1. **Before.** A private programme, the Meridian Trust, built two sites. The Valley is a hardened farm with a pump and a scrubber. The Cradle is a cold vault on the coast holding 3,012 sleepers who paid, or were chosen. Eleven people formed the Wake Group. You were Unit 11, the Director, the only person who could authorise waking the Cradle.
2. **Day 1.** The war. The eleven went under in the valley bunker. Odile Marr, the chief engineer, was never on the register. She stayed awake to keep the lights on. The crossed-out twelfth line on Sam's manifest is hers, struck out at her own request.
3. **Day 212.** Power failing. The valley feed could run the pump and scrubber or the Cradle's cold plant, not both for long. Odile woke you, the Director, to choose.
4. **You chose the valley.** You halved the Cradle's power. That gave it about twelve years. It also gave you twelve years to make the valley big enough to take them in.
5. **Day 300 to 380.** You and Odile woke the ten on schedule. Waking costs memory. The ten wandered out into the ruins confused, with tags on their wrists. (Ledger, day 380: "I count ten in the ruins. One is missing and I know which.")
6. **The sum.** You ran the numbers on the Cradle. A rushed wake kills a lot of them. A late wake kills more. Whatever you picked, you would be the reason people died, and you could not stop doing the arithmetic.
7. **The wipe.** You asked Odile to take it out of you. She did. She left you at the bedroll with a note in your coat and a tag in the depot, then went to the coast to keep the Cradle alive for as long as she could. She has been there ever since.
8. **The radio.** The Cradle's wake protocol never stopped running on its own. The broadcast is the vault calling the Wake Group home. Each night it wakes a few pods and most of them wake wrong. They walk toward the signal. They are the Husks. The broadcast also pulls in drifters, and the raiders come in behind them.
9. **Where we are now.** At the Ash Tower your tag answered the radio: "Eleven tags, one answering." The Cradle now knows its Director is alive and close.

## The plan you left (withheld until chapter three)

"Plant the seeds. Keep them alive. They matter more than you do."

(Chapter Three decisions, 2026-11-01, are in `docs/STORY.md`. The case holds the plan below, the Director code and the redacted minutes of the vote.)

The seeds are not only food. Marta's sealed case holds the Long Table, a staged plan to receive the sleepers as the valley grows. The more beds, food, water and defence the settlement has, the more sleepers can be woken safely. The base-building is the answer to the story. Capacity decides the ending.

## Cast

- **You, Unit 11, the Director.** Quiet competence, no heroics. Past you is heard through log entries. The only thing present you shares with them is the handwriting.
- **Odile Marr, the Warden.** Chief engineer, sixties, never on the register. The voice on the tape. Dry, tired, exact. She did the wipe because you asked, and she made a promise: she may answer questions, but she may not volunteer the reason. So you have to ask the right ones. That is the dialogue mechanic.
- **The Listeners.** Drifters who followed the broadcast to the coast and think it is a rescue. They sing the call sign. Mostly harmless at first. Their leader, the Cantor, is an ex-Meridian comms tech who was also never on the register and wants the vault opened by force. Main antagonists from chapter three.
- **The Husks.** Failed wakes. Slow, heavy, and still people. Boone's old crewmate is one of them.

## Chapter two: The Tidewrack

### Getting there: "Do Not Take the Stairs"

The stairs beneath the Ash Tower bunker lead into the Spine, a forty-tile service tunnel that is the Cradle's back road. It is dark. Three relay stations need restarting to bring the lights up, and there are Husk nests along the way. The broadcast cable runs the whole length, so the radio is louder with every step. The tunnel comes out at Lamp Cove.

### The area

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| Lamp Cove | A tiny harbour and a lighthouse. Odile lives in the lamp room. |
| Saltmere | A drowned boardwalk town. Streets flood and clear with the tide. The records office is under the high-water line. |
| The Wreck Fields | A ship graveyard. Good salvage, nasty crabs, and the best view of the Breakwater. |
| Chapel Row | The Listeners' camp. Hymns, hand-lettered signs, a lot of tin cans on strings. |
| The Breakwater | A concrete sea wall with a vault door set into it. The Cradle. |

### New mechanics

- **Tides.** High and low about every six game hours. Flooded streets block you at high tide, and low tide opens salvage and shortcuts. The water carries radiation, so wading costs you.
- **Sea fog.** Short sight lines some mornings. It hides ambushes and hides loot too.
- **The Questions board.** A new page in the journal. Open mysteries sit there as questions ("Who is the voice on the tape?"). Finding a document, a card or a survivor's answer pins a clue to one. Some questions Odile will only answer if you ask them in the right order.
- **Quorum.** The vault door needs your tag, Odile's voice, and a quorum of tags. Six of the eleven opens it. Every Tag Card you carry counts as one tag, so the survivors' stories you finished in chapter one decide how easy this is. All eleven unlocks something extra at the end.

### The three missions

**Mission 4: Do Not Take the Stairs.** Go under the Ash Tower, restart three relays, and survive the Spine. Clues: your own handwriting on the relay panels ("Pull this one last"), and Director Log 1.

**Mission 5: The Lamplighter.** Meet Odile at the lighthouse. She expects you. She does not say much, and what she does say is careful. She sends you to Saltmere to recover the Manifest Case from the flooded records office, which can only be reached at low tide. Inside: the full manifest with the twelfth name, and Director Logs 2 and 3. Clues: the third log reveals the last two lines of the tape were recorded in your voice.

**Mission 6: Quorum.** Gather the tags and bring Odile to the Breakwater. The Listeners hold the outer door. Three ways through: talk to the Cantor, take the drainage tunnel from the Wreck Fields, or fight. Inside is the antechamber, and the set piece.

### New creatures

- **Wrack crab.** Slow, armoured, heavy damage. Weak from behind.
- **The Drowned.** A Husk variant that rises out of the shallows at high tide.
- **Listener zealot.** Human. Fights with a bell on a pole. Many Listeners can be talked down rather than killed.

### The set piece and the ending of chapter two

The antechamber has a wall of lit panels and one console. It reads:

> WAKE QUORUM: 7 OF 11. DIRECTOR PRESENT. BEGIN STAGE ONE? 3,012 SLEEPERS. PROJECTED LOSS: 41 PERCENT.

(Superseded: quorum was set at six of eleven, and the built console reads "WAKE QUORUM 6 OF 11, PROJECTED LOSS ON A PARTIAL WAKE: 41 PERCENT". See Revision 2 and the Tidewrack progress update.)

The player can walk up to it. They cannot press anything yet, because the console wants a Director code you do not remember. Odile says, "Not yet." Then a recording starts, in your voice:

> "Hello, me. If the number on the screen scares you, good. It scared me. Don't press anything. Go home and ask Marta what's in the case. Then do the sum again. This time you've got a valley to help."

Chapter two ends with the player walking back out into the fog, with a dying vault behind them and the answer waiting at home.

## Chapters three and four, in outline

**Chapter three: The Long Table.** The answer is at home. Marta's case is under the greenhouse and holds the plan. The Listeners find the valley. Priya's nightly replies to the radio are the reason the raiders aim at you. The Cantor wants the vault opened now. You and the ten decide what the valley can hold.

**Chapter four: Capacity.** The Cradle's power runs out. You choose how many sleepers to wake, and the settlement you built decides how many you can keep alive.

### Endings (sketch)

- **The Long Table.** Staged wakes as the settlement grows. Needs camp level 6 and all eleven tags. The best ending and the slowest.
- **The Ark.** Wake as many as the valley can hold now, and let the rest sleep on. The number is set by your beds, food and water.
- **Mercy.** Shut the vault down quietly. No Husks, and no one wakes.
- **The Cantor's way.** Force it open. Fast, loud and terrible.

## How we build it

The new area is the hard part, not the story. Two ways in:

- **A. A second map, swapped in through the Spine (recommended).** The tunnel is the natural place to hide a load. It means a level-swap system for tiles, objects, fog, minimap and spawns. Most of the work. Does not need the main map to change, so old saves keep working.
- **B. Carve it out of an unused patch of the existing map.** Much cheaper, but the Tidewrack would feel cramped and the Spine would be a teleport.

Suggested build order: the Questions board and Quorum first, since they are the story machinery. Then the level swap and the Spine. Then Lamp Cove and Saltmere with tides. Then the Breakwater. If time runs short, cut sea fog first and tides second, and keep the missions.

## The dark layer: who is in the Cradle

The Cradle holds 3,012 sleepers. The brochure said they were the continuity of civilisation, which was true as far as it went. Once you can read the roster, it sorts into three groups:

- **The Architects (about forty).** They signed the deals that made the war possible, then bought themselves a way out of it.
- **The Beneficiaries (about six hundred).** They paid for places and never asked what for.
- **The Rest (about 2,400).** Staff, medics, cooks, engineers, their families. Children. They were handed the same brochure.

Past you found the roster on day 212. **Decision for you:** was the Director one of the Architects? I recommend yes, by signature. Past you signed the continuity instrument that traded the war's conditions for places in the vault, took it for insurance, and learned what it was for when the lights started going out. That makes the wipe about guilt as well as arithmetic, and it gives "They matter more than you do" a second meaning. (Confirmed in round two: yes.)

It also makes the capacity problem much worse. The Cradle can only wake as many sleepers as the settlement can keep alive, so the question is not whether to wake them. It is who.

### The Roster (the late-game centrepiece)

In the final act the player reads the roster: names, jobs, evidence files for the Architects, and a note on each of The Rest. They choose who is woken, and the world remembers. The ten vote with their feet. The Listeners respond. The Husks, who were failed wakes, were on this list too.

## Choices run through everything

1. Every quest has at least one fork that changes something you can see later.
2. Choices cost something. There is no free good option.
3. The game remembers. Reputation tracks: Mercy against Ruthlessness, Trust with the ten, and standing with each faction (the Listeners, the Hull, the raiders).
4. People can die, leave, or turn on you, including members of the ten.
5. The journal gets a page called "What you did", a plain log of the choices that matter.

Early examples: spare or finish a wounded raider. Share rations with a starving traveller or keep them. Take a survivor in over capacity. Tell Wren what the marks mean, or let her keep guessing. Tell Priya about the signal.

**Systems this needs:** a dialogue engine (nodes, options, conditions, effects), story flags, reputation tracks, and the consequence log. Writing should live in a plain script file you can edit.

## Tone and art (18+)

The world is bleak and the people in it are still dry and funny, because that is what people do. The dryness goes against the grain of the world and makes it hurt more.

# Revision 3: wasteland feel, bosses and betrayal

## The Fallout question

The direction you described is a strong one, and the feel is worth leaning into: mutated wildlife, an apex predator, human predators worse than the animals, a power-suit endgame, vault-like facilities, terminals full of dead people's logs. The question is whether to borrow the names and lore as well, or only the feel.

Fallout is Bethesda's. Non-commercial fan projects have generally been tolerated, but that is their call to change, and it would not extend to selling or monetising anything built on it. I am not a lawyer, so treat this as a risk call, not legal advice. My recommendation is to keep the feel and use our own names:

- The story we have written (the Meridian Trust, the Cradle, the eleven tags, Odile, the Roster) is original, and it is stronger than anything we would get by plugging into existing canon. Folding it into Fallout lore would mean explaining it away.
- Real Boston is free to use. The Charles, Bunker Hill, Fenway, the harbour and the bridges belong to nobody. What Fallout 4 owns is its characters, factions and plot, not the city. A post-war Boston setting, with our own factions, is on the table for later.
- Names are the cheapest place to be original, and the most expensive to change late. The game already uses three Fallout names (Rad-Away, Caps, Rad tabs), so I would rename those now.

## Betrayal side missions

Template, built in "The Kind Stranger":

1. A likeable stranger asks for help. Their story is slightly too clean.
2. A skill check lets you notice. The tell is physical, not a pop-up.
3. If you notice, you can arrive armed and warned, and the camp opens with an alarm. If you do not, you are walked into a trap, lose your gear, and wake up in a cage.
4. You escape with what you can find, recover your things, and fight through to a kingpin.
5. The ending is yours: pay him off, kill him, take what he has, or let him walk. He remembers.

More of these, with different shapes: a trader who poisons the well, a doctor who experiments, a settler offered "refuge" in a labour camp, a friendly raider who wants a favour you would regret, a stranger who is honest and gets killed for it while you are still deciding whether to trust her.

**Farming perks (by level).** Extra harvest chance rises each level. Level 4 gives a bonus seed on every harvest. Level 6 gives hardy crops, which grow on a night they were not watered about a third of the time. Level 8 gives market eye, which sells produce for 15% more.

**Cooking perks.** Meal buffs last 5% longer per level, and the extra-serving chance still rises with level. Level 7 gives chef's cut, which sells cooked dishes for 20% more.

**New dishes.** Roast corn and bean mash (campfire). Corn chowder, pumpkin stew, melon juice and healroot tonic (stove, gated by cooking level). Jerky and dried fish (drying rack), which keep for ever and carry no radiation.

**Irrigation.** The sprinkler waters every tilled tile within three tiles, and it runs only while connected to a purifier through the pipe network. Buried pipes run underground and can pass under walls, floors and whole buildings, joining up with surface pipes. A coloured light on the sprinkler shows green when connected and red when not. Press X on a buried pipe tile to dig it up.

**Building options.** Tile floor, concrete floor, carpet and thatch roof, plus the drying rack.

**Merchants** now stock the new seeds.

## Progress update: balance pass and the Tidewrack (Chapter Two playable)

**Balance pass.** Measured against a simple model of player power (health, weapon damage per second, armour, healing items) rather than by feel.

- Boss fights were the main problem. At the old numbers a player lasted about 5 seconds in a fight that took about 20 seconds to win. Bosses now hit for less and have more health, and their damage barely rises with level. A prepared player (armour and about four healing items) should now finish a boss fight with a little to spare, and an unprepared one still loses.
- Normal enemies scale more gently with level (health +5% per level, damage +2.5%), and bosses stay a few levels above you. A Kingpin at your level 5 is now level 8 to 10.
- Crop values now climb with tier: radish 1.7, potato 1.8, corn 2.3, glowmelon 3.4, beans 3.5, tomato 4.0, pumpkin 4.9, healroot 6.5 coins per tile per day. Healroot was an outlier at over twice the next best, so it was cut.
- Closed an exploit: jerky made from cheap raw meat was worth twelve times its ingredients. It is now about two and a half times.
- Farming experience on harvest now scales with crop tier, so the level 5 and level 7 gates do not become a wall.

**The Tidewrack.** Reached through the sealed hatch at the end of the Spine, once all three relays are up.

- **Tides.** A twelve-hour cycle of six hours high and six low. At high tide the Wreck Fields are under water and impassable, and a tide that comes in while you are standing there throws you up the shingle and costs you some health. A readout shows the tide and when it turns.
- **Sea fog** drifts across the whole coast.
- **Lamp Cove.** A lighthouse and Odile Marr, who answers only what she is asked. A branching question hub: who she is, what the Cradle is, what you did, the Listeners, the case, the quorum, and one question that needs Memory 2.
- **Mission 5, The Lamplighter.** Odile asks for a Manifest Case from the wreck fields at low tide. It holds Director Logs 2 and 3, which play as flashbacks when read.
- **Saltmere** (a drowned harbour town with a terminal), the **Wreck Fields**, **Chapel Row** and the **Breakwater**.
- **Mission 6, Quorum.** Carry six Tag Cards, get past the Listeners, and read the console at the Cradle door. Five ways past the Listeners: fight them, return the tags (needs Listener reputation 2), lie to them (works if they like you a little, otherwise it turns into a fight), tell them the truth (needs Memory 3), or crawl under them through the culvert at low tide.
- **The console.** With fewer than six tags it refuses. With six it reports a quorum of six of eleven and a projected loss of 41 percent on a partial wake, then plays a recording of the Director: do not open the door yet, go home and ask Marta what is in the case.
- **New creatures:** wrack crab, the Drowned, Listener zealot, and a boss, the Wrack Mother. She screams at half health and calls her young. She drops a crab shell vest.

**Still to do from here.** Chapter Three (Marta's case, the long table), the Hull town, Deepcut and the other survivors' dungeons with proper scripted content, a light and torch system, status effects and chems, companions, and the Cantor.

## Progress update: The Hull, the town

**The place.** A container ship aground in the marsh, with a town built around it. Enter by the gangway in the marsh. The map fills almost the whole screen area: the ship stacked with containers across the north, a boulevard, a central avenue, a plaza, a second street of cargo homes, a dock and the sea to the south. Streetlamps light the night. The town gets properly dark after 9pm.

**Buildings (15 you can enter, 2 shuttered for later).** Every building is its own map behind a loading fade that shows its name.

| District | Buildings |
|---|---|
| North row | Mags' General Store, The Armoury, Plate and Patch (armourer), The Drip Apothecary, The Rusty Anchor (cantina), The Berth Inn, Tinkers' Guildhall |
| The superstructure | Council Hall |
| South row | The Hull Clinic, Stockyard and Seed, Gannet Scrap, Harbour Guard HQ, Reclaimers' Lodge |
| Shuttered (future) | The Archive, The Pit |
| Cargo row | Eight cargo homes |

Guild halls and HQs have a shop and meeting floor with a private bedroom for every resident.

**People.** 37 residents, and every one has a job, a workplace, a bed in their own home, and a routine tied to the game clock. Shopkeepers open at 8, close at 18, drink at the cantina, go home, and sleep from 22 to 6. The barkeep works nights and sleeps by day. Night guards take the gate and plaza after dark and sleep in the barracks by day. Dockhands work the dock. Retirees and gossips sit in the plaza. You can watch them commute between buildings along the street, and enter a house at night to find them asleep (they will not talk until morning). Four drifters pass through on a three-day rotation.

**Trade.** Ten vendors with their own stock that refreshes each day: general goods, weapons, armour, medicine, seeds and animals, scrap, meals, blueprints and guild kit, reclaimer gear, guard issue. Vendors pay a premium for what they deal in (the stockyard for farm goods, the scrapyard for materials, and so on). Hull reputation lowers prices and unlocks better stock (weapons at 3, bone and combat gear at 2 to 4).

**Services.** The doctor patches you up (25 caps) or clears radiation (35). The inn rents a bunk for 15 caps and sleeps you until morning. The barkeep and the reclaimers sell rumours that mark real places on your map. Drifters can be hired as settlers (40 caps, needs a free bed).

**Job board.** Six jobs in the plaza, each hands out caps, items, experience and Hull reputation: Rats in the Hold, Water Run, A Brute at the Wall, Milk and Eggs, Bones for the Gate, Scout the Deepcut.

**Built to grow.** Buildings, residents, vendors and missions are all data. New guild halls and faction HQs only need a row in the building list. Interior layouts are generated from the building type. Rooms for future residents already exist (spare bedrooms in every guild and HQ). The two shuttered buildings are ready for the Archive (lore and records) and the Pit (arena fights and betting).
